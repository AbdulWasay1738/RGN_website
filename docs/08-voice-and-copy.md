# 08. Voice and copy rules (from the client)

These apply to anything you write that a visitor or the client will read: alt text, error messages, empty states, Studio help text and the editor guide. Marketing copy itself comes verbatim from the prototype.

1. **No em dashes.** Use a full stop, a comma or a colon. No fluff and no hype.
2. **Active voice, present tense.** Avoid passive constructions, unnecessary "-ly" adverbs and "-ing" gerunds, and past tense where possible. Prefer strong, direct verbs.
3. **Team perspective.** "Our team at Reel Good News", "we help", "our approach". Jenna appears as founder and subject-matter expert, not as the centre of every message.
4. **Fewer "you" and "your".** Lead with the value or outcome. Use declarative statements. For example, write "Receive a tailored recommendation and 10 quick wins.", not "You get a recommendation and a proposal that fits."
5. **Concise.** Cut redundant words from headlines, subheadlines and lists. Every word has a job.
6. **Say what Reel Good News can influence.** Build credibility, establish authority, communicate expertise, strengthen relationships, earn customer confidence. Avoid "trust" as a generic theme (never on Careers) and never promise that audiences will "believe" someone. Never promise results. The site's position: we guarantee a clear process, defined deliverables and honest measurement.
7. **The Go-to-Market Strategy (`reference/client-brief/GTM Strategy.md`) is the source of truth** for positioning, audience (founders and marketing leaders at B2B and technology companies with complex sales) and value proposition.
8. **Typography rule** (see 03): one typeface per headline. Benedict for accents only.
9. **Never mention the assessment's bonus video** on the site or in automated messages.
10. **Content packages never show prices** anywhere: pages, FAQ, page source or metadata.
11. Keep the placeholders in square brackets (for example `[number] business days`) until the client supplies the value.
